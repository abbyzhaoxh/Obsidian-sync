	- The course is about **variation in the strategies by which different languages encode semantics (stated in terms of functions) into syntax and morphology**
	- Dependency syntax
		- much more easily applicable to any language
		- the notion that linguistic units, e.g. words, are connected to each other by directed links. The (finite) verb is taken to be the structural center of clause structure
		- notice the pattern, describe it
	- Parts-of-Speech Systems
		- closed word class eg. prep. in English, limited number
		- open word class eg. noun, verb, new words will add to it
	- Leipzig Glossing Rules
		- Word-by-word alignment
		- Morpheme-by-morpheme correspondence
	- Universal Dependency (UD) framework
		- language material or its transliteration will be in *italics* (in addition to original orthography) 
		- as by the Leipzig Glossing Rules, glosses of content morphemes will be in standard font, whereas glosses of function morphemes will be in small caps (COP, ACC, 3SG)

	- The verb
		- ![[Pasted image 20251021133823.png]]
		- ![[Pasted image 20251021134021.png]]
		- **infinitive**: a verb used as an argument, not the main predicate(谓语), behaves like a noun phrase
			- eg. I like *to read*.
		- **participle** (分词): a verb used to modify a noun, like an adj (NP=noun phrase), "verbal adj"
			- eg. a *broken* window
		- **converb**: also modify a noun, like adv
		- ![[Pasted image 20251021134429.png]]
		- ![[Pasted image 20251021134707.png]]
		- **verbal noun**: fully become a noun, can take case marking, determiners
			- eg. *The singing of the birds* was lovely. (It can take oblique marking *of the birds*)
		- ![[Pasted image 20251022153850.png]]
		- **Supine**: a special infinitive-like form that expresses purpose or destination
		- **Gerundive**: a secondary participle expressing necessity, obligation or desirability
